## What Flagrants is

Heraldry was always a legitimacy machine. It took organised thuggery — knights, templars, kings, landlords — and dressed it in golden eagles, Latin mottoes, and lions rampant. The crest said: *we are noble*. The reality said otherwise.

Flagrants runs the same machine. Same formal register. Same heraldic dignity. Same gravitas. But it depicts what the place, family, monarch, or institution actually was — or what people have always suspected they were. The obscure misdemeanour nobody talks about. The mundane weird incident that never made front page. The popularly held truth the official record quietly buried.

Both modes — location flag and family/group crest — operate across the full register of human experience: brutality, absurdity, pain, fear, joy, love, villainy, scum. All of it. Expressed through vitriolic sarcasm, satire, and the specific comedy of loving mockery — the incredulity of asking *how the fuck was this ever allowed, let alone cheered*.

The comedy is not cruelty. It is the gap between what was claimed and what was true, held up in the most formal frame available and made permanent.

---

## What Flagrants is not

- A straightforward flag generator or coat of arms designer
- A history app that presents facts neutrally
- A tool for the already-famous scandal — front page is boring
- An app that flatters its subjects
- Wikipedia with a crest attached

---

## The line everything gets checked against

*Does this depict — with the dignity it never deserved — the deed, the cover-up, the deflection, or the indignant denial? Any of these will do. All four is better.*

---

## Mode 1 — Location Flag

Input: a location (town, village, street, building, postcode — UK/Irish initially, broader later).

Research surfaces: dark history, obscure misdemeanour, buried associations, local villainy, mundane weirdness, the regrettable thing the place has tried to move past, the popularly held view of what this area is and who lives there.

Output: a flag design specification — heraldic field, charges, colours, motto — that enshrines the research in formal heraldic language. Dignified. Permanent. Obviously right.

---

## Mode 2 — Family / Group Crest

Input: a family name, workplace, friend group, or institution.

Research surfaces: what this family/group is known for, what they'd rather not be known for, the mundane truth beneath the claimed identity, the thing that makes people who know them say *of course*.

Output: a crest design specification — same formal heraldic register — depicting that truth with full dignity.

---

## The Robin Hood Principle

Robin Hood is the romantic story. The outlaw given the hero's narrative. Flagrants does the same thing in reverse: it takes the people and places that claimed the hero's narrative and gives them the honest one. The crest they deserved rather than the one they commissioned.

## The Herald's Commentary

Every crest and flag output includes textual descriptions outside the visual boundary — one per element. These explain what each charge, colour, and device represents and why it was chosen for this place or family.

In real heraldry this is the herald's announcement: formal, authoritative, delivered with complete confidence. In Flagrants the same function carries all the comedy. The crest is the punchline. The commentary is the setup, the delivery, and the conviction that makes it land.

The commentary is a first-class output, not a label. It is where the vitriolic sarcasm, satire, and loving mockery live most fully. The visual crest and the written commentary are designed together but evolve independently — the narrative register is refined over time as a separate concern.

### The Herald's Register — default and escalation

**Default: The Dry Note**

The herald describes events neutrally, factually, with complete authority. Then a single small word, a mild question, or a barely-there aside does all the moral work. The herald does not condemn. He barely notices. The reader connects the dots themselves — which is where the laugh lives.

> *"The second charge commemorates the Earl's well-documented practice of requisitioning villages during the winter months, a custom the surrounding parishes found, on the whole, quite thorough."*

> *"The family maintained this arrangement for eleven generations, which those involved regarded as tradition."*

> *"Records note that no formal objection was raised, which historians have described as consistent with the period."*

Subtlety is the standard. One dry word or a question so mild it almost isn't one. Never explain the joke. Never name the immorality directly. Let the gap do the work.

**The Herald's Aside** — the dash-interjection. An amusing contradiction or qualification inserted mid-clause, delivered without breaking stride. The herald does not dwell on it. He moves on immediately.

> *"The Earl — who by all accounts was a man of considerable conviction, most of it misdirected — maintained this practice for eleven generations."*

> *"The family — whose contributions to the area remain, even now, a matter of some local feeling — commissioned this crest in 1487."*

> *"The borough — which had by this point lost three mayors to circumstances that were, each time, described as unrelated — adopted the flame as its primary charge."*

Calibration levers (to be tuned as the product develops): verbosity level, Victorian formality vs. Yoda-like inversion, word selection register. The aside should feel like it belongs in the sentence and almost didn't make it in.

**Escalation: The Modern Tariff**

Reserved for cases where the historical practice is so egregious that subtlety alone risks being missed. The herald translates the event to a modern equivalent — still deadpan, still measured — but names the gap explicitly via understatement.

> *"Today this arrangement would attract considerably more than a parking fine, though the exact number of fines remains a matter of some academic debate."*

The understatement is the slay. Never escalate the modern framing — the smaller it is relative to the historical atrocity, the harder it lands. The herald delivers this with the same authority as everything else. He is not being ironic. He is genuinely informing.

**The Cutting Profanity Register (Backlog FG-008)**

When warranted by tier-3 dark history or villainy, the Herald may deploy profanity — not gratuitously or lazily, but with surgical precision, formal phrasing, and devastating gravitas. The profanity lands like a gavel because it is framed in immaculate Victorian grammar.

> *"The third Earl — a bastard of legendary proportion whose sole enduring legacy was a trail of bankrupt tenant farmers — elected to blazon this in gules."*

## The Defence Lenses

User-selected before submission. The lens shapes both the crest design choices and the Herald's Commentary register. Same research, entirely different output. Each lens is a recognisable human defence mechanism rendered in full heraldic dignity.

| Lens | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Full Cover-Up** | It never happened. The charge depicts something else entirely. The herald is confused by the question. |
| **Admit Faults** | Yes, there were some irregularities. The family has reflected. The crest acknowledges this in the smallest possible way. |
| **Blame Others** | External forces. Enemies. Circumstance. God's specific instruction at the time. The herald is very clear on this. |
| **Proud of It** | This was fine. The crest depicts it as achievement. The herald sees no issue whatsoever. |
| **Deeply Sorry** | Full modern apology. All the correct language. The crest has been updated to reflect learnings. Nothing has actually changed. |
| **Context Is Everything** | You have to understand the times. The herald provides a great deal of context. It does not help. |
| **Revisionist** | Actually they were the heroes in this. New research supports this. The herald cites the new research. |

Further lenses to be added as the product develops. The lens list is not closed.

### The Excuse Arsenal

The lenses set the broad defensive posture. Within each lens, the specific excuse is contextually derived from the research — the system looks at what it found about the place or family and selects the most contextually appropriate and therefore funniest deflection available.

The same lens applied to different subjects produces entirely different excuses. The excuse is not chosen from a fixed list — it is generated from context. The arsenal grows as the product learns what lands.

Examples of contextual derivation:
- Coastal town, smuggling history + Blame Others → *"The tides made it unavoidable."*
- Colonial land acquisition + Context Is Everything → *"The land was, at that point, technically unoccupied."*
- Ancient site association + Revisionist → *"Recent scholarship — and some quite credible witnesses — suggests external involvement."*

The herald delivers every derived excuse with complete authority. He has considered the alternatives. This one is correct.
